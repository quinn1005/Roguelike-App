# Mint Condition changelog

## v1.3.0

Money is tight again, and packs look the part.

- **Cards are worth far less.** A pack's cards are now worth about half its price on a typical pull, so packs are a gamble rather than an investment. Raw card prices no longer balloon for mint copies (slabs still carry the big grade premiums), and holos are worth 3× instead of 4×. The best pulls (mythic full art holos) top out at a few hundred dollars instead of thousands.
- **Lou rations packs.** Three packs a customer a night, two during the boom.
- **Lou's till.** Lou only pays out of the cash in his register each night (about $50, more in the boom, less in the crash). Cards worth more than that have to go to the Saturday auction.
- **New pack art.** Every booster is now a crimped foil wrapper with the line's logo in its own lettering, a window showing the set's mythic, a holo "Mythics inside" seal and a foil sheen. The new packs are used in the shop and when you tear one open.
- The Collector ending now needs a binder worth $400 (or the Ghost Print).

## v1.2.0

Every card is its own card.

- **Grim Grocers redrawn by hand.** All 30 cards now have their own produce and a costume that fits the name: Ghoulash is a pot of stew, The Corn Reaper is an ear of corn with a scythe, Sir Squashalot wears a helmet and carries a shield, Radish Ripper carries a knife, Plague Plum wears a beak mask, and the ghosts (Banshee Banana, Poltergrape, Ghostato, Haunted Parsnip) are the produce itself turned see-through, not a sheet thrown over fruit. Fifteen new produce shapes, new costumes, held props and three new backdrops (lab, cornfield, cellar).
- **No more repeats in the other sets.** Each set has a long list of subjects, and every card gets its own until the list runs out. Names come from the subject, so a card called Space Pigeon shows a pigeon. Where a subject does come back, it is drawn as a pair, a close-up, a group or from far off, in a different setting and colors.
- **New subjects:** Bloomlings turtles, fawns, jellies and golems; Moth & Lantern ladybirds, scorpions, grasshoppers, stick insects, snails and chrysalises; Tidebound rays, nautiluses, starfish and squid; Rift Duelists liches, chimeras, harpies and titans; Grimoire Accord imps, owl familiars, giants and shades; six new Hollow Pantheon gods (30 symbols, one per card); Ironclad Derby motorbikes, sleds, walkers, boats, balloons and drills racing through deserts, cities, snow, bridges and canyons; Cryptid Field Notes worms, dog men, robed visitors, lizard men, blobs and the Hat Man; Starpost moons, telescopes, aliens, galaxies, suns and a space pigeon.

## v1.1.0

A visual overhaul and a much bigger card pool.

- **A grimier world.** Film grain, dirt and a dark vignette over every screen, drained and dirtier city scenes, worn buttons, yellowed coffee-stained paper and a flickering ceiling light. The cards are left untouched and glow faintly: they are the only bright, happy thing in the room.
- **30 cards in every set.** All eleven lines now have 30 cards: 14 common, 9 uncommon, 5 rare and 2 mythic rare (marked ✦). That makes 330 cards to collect.
- **Redrawn card art.** Every card now has its own design. Neon Saints wrestlers have real bodies (heavy, muscle, lanky, small and giant), poses, outfits, props and arenas. Bloomlings come in eleven body plans (pups, birds, serpents, toads, slimes, beetles, drakes, fish, sprouts, bats) and three new elements: storm, frost and shadow. Every other line gained new creatures, figures, vehicles and scenes.
- **Holo for every card.** Any card can now turn up holo, with a rainbow sheen and fine glitter. Rarer cards are holo more often.
- **Full art mythics.** Mythic rares also come as Full Art prints, where the illustration spills across the whole card, and as Full Art Holo, the rarest pull in the game. Packs have a small chance of a mythic in the rare slot.
- New codex entry and two new achievements: Mythic pull and Full shine.
- Fixed: submissions could favor the first card in each set.

## v1.0.0

The full game: five weeks at Gemline Authentication.

- **The whole quarter.** 25 working days in five weeks: Onboarding, Expansion, The Boom, The Crash and Reckoning. Rent is now due every Friday and rises with the boom; pay jumps 25% in the boom and is cut 25% in the crash, and card prices follow the market.
- **Eight new card lines** with their own art, backs and tells: Moth & Lantern (hidden UV ink), Tidebound (water test), Rift Duelists (hologram on the tilt stand), Grimoire Accord (Alpha corners), Hollow Pantheon (gold-leaf tarnish), Ironclad Derby (print-run stats), Cryptid Field Notes (registry serials) and Starpost (perforation counts and the inverted error).
- **Six new tools:** tilt stand, dropper, registry terminal, precision scale, microscope and light box. Ring forgeries are perfect except for the microprint along the bottom border.
- **Slabs.** Graded cards are sealed into a PSA-style slab with a printed label, grade and barcode. Rejected and returned cards leave with a sticky note.
- **Subtle holo swirls.** The loupe now shows the natural spiral of the foil instead of arrows; you read the direction from its curl.
- **Meters.** Reputation (raises your pay and your options), Suspicion (too high and you're arrested) and a hidden Integrity.
- **Story.** Carrow's escalating walk-ins and Pike's cut, a VIP who wants a 10, a reporter collecting evidence, a widower, a brick through the window, Carrow's ring deal and his partnership offer. Seven endings: Whistleblower, Kingpin, Head Grader, The Collector, Still Standing, Fired, Evicted, Arrested.
- **The Ghost Print hunt.** Four clues hidden on cards, each visible only with the right tool, lead to Ellis Marsh's one-of-one.
- **Collecting.** Apex Grading on Mill Street (mail-in slabs, mistakes you can dispute), the Saturday auction at the Corn Exchange (live bidding against collectors, consign your own cards), set completion rewards, and a Fake Binder of confiscated counterfeits.
- **Codex, achievements and settings.** A codex of every tell you've learned, 24 achievements kept across runs, music and effects volume, and shift speed. Shifts save mid-way and resume where you left off.

## v0.3.0

Sound, a bigger desk, and pack openings worth opening.

- Music and ambience on every screen, generated live: a noir title theme, office hum and typing at the bulletin, a ticking clock at the desk that turns tense after 4 pm, dread under the letters, rain and traffic on the street, an old jazz radio at Lou's, wind chimes at the night market, a jukebox at the diner, and a music box at home. Sound effects for tools, tags, stamps, citations and cash. Mute button in the top corner.
- Pack openings are rebuilt: tear along the seam, watch the foil fly off, and flip cards one at a time. Rares shake and glow before they turn over, then hit with light rays, sparks and a fanfare. Holos shimmer, new cards get a NEW badge, and the pull fans out at the end.
- Cards now have separate flaws on the front and the back. Flip every card and check both sides.
- The card is bigger and centered at the desk. On phones the form and worksheet sit in a slim strip under the card that opens into a drawer; on wider screens they sit on either side.
- The loupe no longer runs off the top of the screen.
- Rain at home and at the diner stays outside the window.

## v0.2.0

Evenings are now places you go, and the desk teaches you as you work.

- After your shift you step out onto a rainy street. Tap a building to walk there: Brick & Mortar Cards, the night market, the Last Stop Diner, or home.
- Every location has its own illustrated scene and someone to talk to: Lou behind the counter, the hooded seller at the bins, Sal at the diner, and Theo asleep at home. What they say changes with your choices.
- Working a diner shift now pays tips and lets you overhear gossip that hints at tomorrow's tricks.
- Home is where you open your binder and go to sleep. The evening clock shows how late it's getting.
- Wrong verdicts now print a citation slip immediately, explaining exactly what you missed: which flaw, which counterfeit tell, which form mismatch.
- The reference binder has a Rules tab with every standing rule.
- Each new day opens with a tip about that day's new tool or rule.

## v0.1.0

The first slice: one working week at Gemline Authentication.

- 5 in-game days with a real-time 9-to-5 shift, a daily quota and a morning bulletin whose rules stack up.
- 3 card lines (Grim Grocers, Neon Saints, Bloomlings), 26 cards in all, with holo, shadowless, 1st Edition and misprint variants.
- Grading desk with Hand, Loupe, Ruler, UV lamp and Reference binder. Tag flaws with the loupe; the worksheet builds your grade.
- Five verdicts: Grade, Authentic Error, Altered, Counterfeit, Return to client.
- Counterfeits (blurry print, wrong copyright, UV glow, backwards holo swirl, wrong backs) and altered cards (added stamps, recolored corners, trimming).
- Payday with accuracy bonuses, citations, fines, food and rent. Seven citations and you're fired.
- Evenings: card shop with pack opening, singles and selling; night-market bulk bins; diner shifts; your binder.
- Story: Vince Carrow's bribe, Mina's card, Theo's inhaler, the rent and Friday's audit, with several endings.
